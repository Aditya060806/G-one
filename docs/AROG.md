# AROG



**The individual technologies already exist. Our innovation is addressing the gap between them for continuous, personalized, offline-capable post-discharge monitoring in Indian conditions**



**Q1. WHY HASN'T ANYONE SOLVED IT?**

The problem isn't that health monitoring doesn't exist. The problem is that existing solutions don't necessarily combine continuous monitoring, personalized anomaly detection, offline operation, environmental context, and a complete patient-family-doctor care loop in one low-cost system.



***“Many individual components have already been researched and commercialized. Our challenge is the integration and optimization of those capabilities for the specific constraints of our target environment — affordability, unreliable connectivity, privacy, post-discharge monitoring and healthcare-provider workload.”***



Q2. **THIS ALREADY EXISTS. WHY YOURS?**

Yes, individual parts of our solution already exist. Wearables can measure heart rate, SpO₂ and temperature, and AI-based health monitoring also exists. Our contribution is not inventing the sensor or AI independently. Our focus is integrating these capabilities into an edge-first, offline-capable and personalized post-discharge health companion.



| **Existing approach**                          | **AROG**



| Measures vitals                            | Measures vitals                             |

| Often uses fixed thresholds                | \*\*Personal baseline\*\*                       |

| Cloud-dependent solutions exist            | \*\*Edge-first\*\*                              |

| Internet may be required for remote alerts | \*\*72-hour offline buffering\*\*

| Mainly physiological data                  | \*\*Health + environment\*\*                    |

| Patient-focused                            | \*\*Patient + family + DOCTOR

| Raw measurements                           | \*\*Prioritized alerts\*\*                      |

| Monitoring                                 | \*\*Monitoring → Detection → Alert → Action\*\*





We are also not claiming to be the first wearable health monitor. Our focus is a different use case: continuous post-discharge monitoring, particularly where connectivity, affordability and continuity of care are challenges. We combine local processing, personal baselines, offline buffering and escalation to family and doctors.



***“A smartwatch primarily gives me measurements. AROG is designed as a healthcare continuity system. It establishes an individual's baseline, detects meaningful deviations, processes them locally, continues working offline, incorporates environmental context, and escalates prioritized alerts to the patient, family and doctor. So we're not trying to build another smartwatch; we're trying to close the gap between hospital discharge and continuous care at home. We identified a specific unmet combination of requirements and designed an integrated solution around it.”***

**Q4. So what is the ACTUAL gap?**



*“The gap is not the absence of wearable health monitoring. The gap is continuous, personalized and privacy-preserving health intelligence that can continue operating during connectivity failures and connect the patient with family and healthcare providers after discharge.”*



**Q5. OVERALL**

*"Wearable monitoring and health AI already exist. We are not claiming to invent those technologies. The gap we're targeting is continuous post-discharge care under real-world constraints. Recently discharged patients, elderly people, rural communities and patients affected by disasters can lose continuous healthcare visibility, especially when connectivity is unreliable. AROG combines wearable vital and motion sensing with personalized baseline-based anomaly detection, edge AI, 72-hour offline buffering, environmental risk context and prioritized alerts to patients, families and doctors. So our innovation is the integration of these capabilities into an affordable, privacy-preserving and offline-first care loop rather than simply creating another health wearable.”*
